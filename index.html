01 / 03

Mapping a hallway
from distance readings.

BUILT & TESTED

For a microprocessor systems project, I built a rotating time-of-flight scanner and used it to reconstruct an indoor hallway. The challenge was getting the physical movement, sensor measurements and desktop visualization to agree on what a point in space meant.

  • On the board: ARM Cortex-M4F (MSP432E401Y), GPIO, I²C sensor acquisition and stepper-motor control in C.
  • Over the wire: UART at 115200 bps carried measurements to Python.
  • On the screen: NumPy, PySerial and Open3D transformed angle-distance samples into a 3D spatial representation.

02 / 03

What makes a
processor wait?

SIMULATED & MEASURED

I worked from x86 instruction traces toward a more practical question: how much does a wider processor pipeline actually help?

  • Wrote and ran a script to generate MacSim-compatible Intel Pin traces for 13 benchmark workloads, then checked their raw trace files and instruction fields.
  • Explored pipeline-width configurations using 52 measurements across the benchmark set.
  • Compared scheduler-rate configurations to understand why more execution capacity doesn't always translate to proportionally faster programs.
ENGINEERING NOTE / 02Doubling an architectural limit doesn't double useful work. Instruction dependencies, resource constraints and workload characteristics still decide throughput.

03 / 03

Making sensors
agree on the world.

INTEGRATED & TESTED

On an autonomous electrified vehicle project, I worked with the integration side of robotics: getting sensing, mapping and control modules to operate together on embedded hardware.

  • Brought up LiDAR, RGB-D camera, IMU and wheel-odometry inputs with ROS on NVIDIA Jetson Nano / Linux.
  • Worked on occupancy-grid mapping, LiDAR-based control and debugging the sensing pipeline.
  • Validated behaviour with repeatable scenarios and logged test runs.
ENGINEERING NOTE / 03Robotics is as much about integration as algorithms. A sensor can be individually correct and still produce a bad system-level result when timing, frames or data flow don't align.
